# Airborne sensor cosmetic reference survey (2026-09-29)

Purpose: visual vocabulary for the Palisade pod sensor gate (A12). Cosmetic
only; nothing here is functional or performance data. Photos were viewed on
Wikimedia Commons in the browser pane; **no images are saved in the repo** and
the file names below are only pointers. Observations are the primary model's
own, from the viewed frames.

| Reference (Commons file) | What reads visually |
|---|---|
| AN/AAQ-33 Sniper (`Sniper Advanced Targeting Pod.jpg`, `ANAAQ-33 Sniper XR.JPG`) | Plain long cylinder; the sensor is a **faceted, obliquely cut window** at the nose, not a dome. Body is broken by circumferential panel bands, rivet/screw rows, small hatch plates and a **cooling-air grille and scoop aft**. Sits under a flat adapter/pylon plate. |
| AN/AAQ-28 LITENING (`ANAAQ-28 LITENING.JPG`) | **Ball-turret nose** on a cylinder: a single round seam separates the rotating ball; access plates, latch tabs and stencil blocks along the body; stepped aft section. |
| AN/ASQ-228 ATFLIR (`US.NAVY FA-18F... ATFLIR ... IWAKUNI 20150503.JPG`) | Stepped cylinder: a **rotating hemispherical head** ahead of a fixed body, a seam ring between, mounted on a deep fairing. |
| LAIRCM / DIRCM turret (`LAIRCM pointer-tracker assembly on USAF Boeing KC-46A at ILA-2024.jpg`) | A small **dome sitting in a recessed bezel**, ringed by a **bolt-circle flange** (radial screw rings) with a coloured seal ring and warning labels. The dome itself is split into a sliced/faceted cap. |
| Russian DIRCM head (`Russian DIRCM jammer head at ARMY-2022.jpg`) | **Glass sphere on a metal collar**, with a short cylindrical stub below: a ball-on-neck read. |
| Typhoon PIRATE IRST (`Aeronautica Militare Typhoon IRST (27476944291).jpg`) | A **spherical lens set into a carved scoop** in the fairing; the lens is recessed behind a hood so the fairing and lens read as one form. |
| Rafale OSF IRST (`Rafale OSF ILA 2018.jpg`) | A **flat faceted window** (pentagonal) in a sculpted hump that blends into the skin; thin panel seam lines around it. |

## Patterns worth borrowing

1. Sensors are almost never bare cylinders with a bare hemisphere. The
   convincing ones are **integrated**: a hood, scoop, or recessed bezel makes
   the lens and the body read as one form.
2. **Stepped seams and rings** (a visible turret seam line, a flange with a
   bolt circle) carry most of the "real hardware" read.
3. Windows are often **flat/faceted or obliquely cut**, not round domes; round
   domes appear only on turrets and ball heads.
4. Housings are quiet, low-relief; detail is panel bands, hatches, latch tabs,
   a cooling scoop or grille, and screws, not extra protrusions.
5. Multiple apertures at one end are normally **different types** (a ball
   turret plus a flat window), not several identical domes on stalks.

## Implication for C4-C6 (open, for the user's decision)

The stalk-and-ball masts read as protruding cylinders with domes. The
references suggest keeping the user's three-mount layout and sizes but
changing the vocabulary: fewer free-standing necks, more hooded/recessed
bezels with bolt-circle flanges, and mixing dome, faceted-window and
ball-turret types across the three mounts.
